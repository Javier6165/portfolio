## Design Specificity Verdict

The Home feels authored as a mature Swiss-influenced product-design portfolio, but not yet authored unmistakably for Javier. The 12-column system works as invisible infrastructure; it rarely becomes an expressive compositional rule. Standard patterns repeat: text/photo Hero, four equal snapshot cells, two equal secondary projects, three equal testimonials and split FAQ.

The independent design review and browser evidence agree on the central problem: craft is strong, specificity is not. The automated detector returned zero findings for `app/page.tsx`; this confirms that no obvious anti-pattern rule is firing, but it does not evaluate the imported CSS compositions. No reliable user-visible overlay was available because browser evaluation was read-only. Fallback evidence used desktop/mobile screenshots, computed sizes, contrast and layout metrics.

## Overall Impression

The site is elegant, legible and credible. It currently reads as a very good senior designer portfolio rather than a memorable editorial object. The largest opportunity is to turn the grid from an alignment system into a recognizable house language: recurring datums, deliberate span changes, contrasting densities and a visual grammar derived from rules, states and dependencies.

## What's Working

- The Hero has a strong three-line silhouette; the italic on *calm* is the only memorable typographic gesture and is appropriately restrained.
- Work has a convincing main/secondary hierarchy. The caption grid under LogicX is the closest moment to a designed editorial spread.
- The off-white canvas, dark ink, grayscale portraits and square geometry feel mature. Responsive behavior is robust, with no horizontal overflow at 390px and good contrast throughout.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

Cognitive load is low: the visitor always has one obvious reading path and no decision point exceeds four primary options. The emotional arc peaks in the Hero and the monumental LogicX field, then settles into a long, uniform middle. Testimonials, Lab and FAQ do not create a second peak, while the footer does not yet provide a memorable ending.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The grid is infrastructure rather than identity

**Why it matters:** Equal splits and repeated section recipes make the page feel templated despite precise implementation.

**Fix:** Define three persistent vertical datums and a baseline rhythm. Reuse them across Hero, Work, About and Contact, then cross them deliberately in only one or two high-impact spreads. Replace equal 4×1, 2×1 and 3×1 divisions with controlled 2+1+1, 7/5 and dominant-plus-marginal compositions.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Work and Lab use category-generic abstract plates

**Why it matters:** LOGICX/circles, Aa/modules, 03 and generic charts look like familiar minimal-portfolio placeholders rather than Javier's design intelligence.

**Fix:** Build one visual grammar from the actual domain: rules, dependencies, states, decision paths, constraints and annotations. Use it as a family of editorial plates with different densities, never as fake product evidence.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder`

### [P1] The page has one continuous visual tempo

**Why it matters:** At 5,595px desktop and 7,899px mobile, similar spacing, scale and section headings produce an emotional valley after Work.

**Fix:** Compose a density score: compressed Snapshot, monumental lead case, catalogue-like secondary cases, photographic About pause, one dominant testimonial with marginal voices, technical Lab index and quiet FAQ. Each chapter should have a different silhouette when blurred.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] Typography is refined but not sufficiently authorial

**Why it matters:** Similar weights and predictable measures mean most hierarchy is achieved only by size. The identity relies too heavily on one italic word.

**Fix:** Keep Instrument Sans but exploit more radical scale, width, line-span and caption contrasts. Make functional captions smaller and more exact, and let only one or two additional moments reuse the italic motif. A second typeface is not the first fix.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Mobile becomes a standard vertical feed

**Why it matters:** The editorial signature largely disappears below 720px; the secondary CTA also wraps into an isolated row.

**Fix:** Design a real four-column mobile master: alternating inset/full-width media, narrow caption columns, controlled indent changes and explicit CTA placement.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Design leader scanning for authorship:** The Hero proves taste and the main case proves hierarchy, but the equal snapshot/testimonial/project grids read as safe defaults. They will remember “clean” more readily than a specific design point of view.

**Recruiter scanning in under a minute:** The information path is excellent and nothing obstructs evaluation. The risk is differentiation: abstract work plates do not yet make the portfolio feel more credible than other polished senior portfolios.

**Hiring manager on mobile:** Content remains usable, but the long 7,899px page and repeated visual-to-copy stacking reduce pace. The separated “Meet me in 60 seconds” action and tiny metadata weaken the first impression.

## Minor Observations

- The header wordmark and the Hero identity repeat “Javier Ortiz” within roughly 70px.
- Several captions fall to .75–.8125rem, visually timid on mobile even though contrast passes.
- Hero and About photos are distinct and well cropped; their composition can do more grid work without new imagery.
- Do not add a visible graph-paper overlay. Functional rules, alignment and span changes should reveal the grid.
